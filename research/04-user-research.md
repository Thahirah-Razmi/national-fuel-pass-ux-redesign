# User Research

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the empirical research plan, field-research framework, data-collection instruments, and analysis methodology for the National Fuel Pass (NFP) redesign project.

Its purpose is to define how primary research will be conducted to investigate the experiences of citizens, fuel-station personnel, support personnel, and relevant institutional stakeholders across the NFP service ecosystem.

The research is intended to identify verified user and operational findings that can later inform service requirements, design hypotheses (**DH**), and the React MVP.

At this stage, **primary field research has not yet been conducted**. Therefore, this document does not present fabricated interviews, survey results, observations, participant quotations, or usability findings.

All future findings will be classified using the project's epistemological framework:

* **E — Evidence:** Directly documented, observed, or verified information.
* **I — Inference:** A logical interpretation derived from evidence.
* **RH — Research Hypothesis/Question:** An unverified condition requiring investigation.
* **DH — Design Hypothesis:** A proposed solution requiring validation.
* **TD — Technical Decision:** An engineering decision within the prototype.
* **UNKNOWN:** Information that has not been established or verified.

---

# 2. Research Objectives

The research will pursue five primary objectives.

### 2.1 Understand Registration and Recovery

Investigate how users complete registration, respond to ownership-related registration problems, and recover from changes or loss of registered contact information.

### 2.2 Understand QR Presentation and Verification

Investigate how users present their QR codes at fuel stations, how station personnel perform verification, what happens when verification does not succeed, and what recovery actions are taken.

### 2.3 Understand Quota Comprehension

Investigate how users understand their available and remaining fuel quota, quota-reset timing, and differences between allocation and physical fuel availability.

### 2.4 Understand Support and Escalation

Investigate which problems cause users to seek assistance, which support channels they use, what information they need when requesting assistance, and which cases require escalation.

### 2.5 Understand Accessibility and Operational Context

Investigate differences in experience related to language, digital literacy, device access, user context, and fuel-station operating conditions.

These objectives are research objectives rather than predetermined findings or product requirements.

# 3. Research Questions

The following questions are derived from uncertainties identified during the previous desk-research and stakeholder-analysis phases.

## Domain 1: Registration and Onboarding

**RH-01**

### RQ-01.1

What steps in the registration process do users report as difficult, unclear, or requiring assistance?

### RQ-01.2

How do users with limited digital experience or limited access to digital devices complete registration?

### RQ-01.3

What types of input or validation problems do users encounter when entering identity, vehicle, or chassis information?

### RQ-01.4

What information do users understand as necessary before beginning registration?

---

## Domain 2: Ownership Transfer and Account Recovery

**RH-02**

### RQ-02.1

How do users navigate registration when the vehicle is associated with a previous owner's information?

### RQ-02.2

What actions do users attempt when they change or lose access to their registered mobile number?

### RQ-02.3

What information or documentation do users report needing when resolving ownership or contact-number problems?

### RQ-02.4

Where do users seek assistance when self-service recovery does not resolve the problem?

---

## Domain 3: QR Presentation and Verification

**RH-03**

### RQ-03.1

What methods do users actually use to store and present their QR codes at fuel stations?

Possible observed methods may include:

* active smartphone screen
* saved image or screenshot
* printed representation
* another method observed during research

These are observation categories, **not assumptions that all methods are currently used**.

### RQ-03.2

What physical, environmental, device, or connectivity conditions are observed or reported in situations where QR verification takes longer than expected or does not succeed?

The research should investigate possible contributing conditions rather than assume a specific cause.

### RQ-03.3

What happens when a QR code cannot be verified?

### RQ-03.4

What actions do users and station personnel take following an unsuccessful verification attempt?

---

## Domain 4: Quota Visibility and Comprehension

**RH-04**

### RQ-04.1

How do users understand their available, used, and remaining fuel quota?

### RQ-04.2

How do users understand the current quota-reset schedule documented by the service?

### RQ-04.3

Do users distinguish between their allocated quota and the physical availability of fuel at a station?

### RQ-04.4

Do different vehicle categories create different comprehension or operational questions?

---

## Domain 5: Support and Escalation

**RH-05**

### RQ-05.1

What support channels do users use when they encounter NFP-related problems?

### RQ-05.2

What information do users need before contacting support?

### RQ-05.3

Which issues do users report resolving independently?

### RQ-05.4

Which issues require assistance or escalation?

---

## Domain 6: Accessibility and Digital Literacy

**RH-06**

### RQ-06.1

What devices and access methods do participants use when interacting with the service?

### RQ-06.2

What language do participants prefer when interacting with digital service information?

### RQ-06.3

What accessibility or comprehension barriers, if any, do participants encounter?

### RQ-06.4

Do users seek assistance from other people when completing NFP-related tasks?

If assistance is reported, the research should investigate who provides it, what task they assist with, and why assistance was required.

---

## Domain 7: Fuel-Station Workflow

**RH-07**

### RQ-07.1

What are the observed steps from vehicle arrival through completion of a fueling transaction?

### RQ-07.2

What is the observed approximate duration of transactions, and how does it vary across observed situations?

### RQ-07.3

How is QR verification incorporated into the station workflow?

### RQ-07.4

What happens when QR verification, station equipment, or connectivity does not work as expected?

### RQ-07.5

How does the workflow change when queues become longer or transaction volume increases?

The research should document the observed workflow rather than assume that station personnel perform multiple tasks simultaneously or experience a particular type of workload.

| Quota comprehension                | Understand how users interpret quota information and reset timing                  |

### Secondary Contextual Topics

Where relevant, research may also investigate:

* device and connectivity conditions
* perceived trust and security
* differences between vehicle categories

* OTP/SMS experiences
* differences between fuel-station environments
* regional or location-related variation

These topics are exploratory and will only influence the design if supported by research evidence.

# 5. Participant Groups

The research will seek perspectives from different groups involved in the NFP service.

## 5.1 Citizen and Vehicle Users

### Group A — Everyday Private Vehicle Users

Users of privately operated vehicles such as motorcycles, cars, and vans.

### Group B — Commercial / Daily-Income Users

Users who depend on vehicles for income-generating activities, where accessible.

### Group C — Heavy Transport / Agricultural Users

Users operating buses, lorries, agricultural vehicles, or other relevant vehicle categories, where accessible.

### Group D — Users with Different Levels of Digital Access

Users with varying levels of digital experience, device access, age, or confidence using online services.

This group is intended to investigate differences rather than assume that all such users experience barriers.

### Group E — Ownership / Recovery Cases

Users who have experienced vehicle ownership changes, registration conflicts, or changes/loss of registered contact information.

### Group F — Other Relevant Users

Other relevant service users may be included if encountered during recruitment, such as temporary or foreign users.

Their inclusion is exploratory and subject to relevance and access.

---

## 5.2 Fuel-Station Participants

Where access is available:

* Forecourt attendants
* Station managers or supervisors
* Station owners/dealer representatives

The research will first establish what responsibilities and tools each participant actually uses rather than assuming a standardized station workflow.

---

## 5.3 Institutional Participants

Where access is realistically available:

* GIC/NFP support personnel
* Ministry representatives
* Department of Motor Traffic (DMT) representatives
* CPC/LIOC ecosystem representatives
* ICTA representatives where relevant to historical or technical context

Institutional access is not assumed.

Where direct access cannot be obtained, authoritative public documentation may be used instead.

# 6. Research Methods

The research will use multiple methods because different methods reveal different aspects of the service.

| Method                     | Primary Purpose                                | Can Establish                                                                        | Cannot Establish                                          |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Semi-structured interviews | Experiences, perceptions, reported workarounds | Reported experiences, perceptions, explanations, recalled events                     | Actual population frequency or directly observed behavior |
| Contextual inquiry         | Behavior in context                            | Observed actions and contextual explanations                                         | Nationally representative statistics                      |
| Fuel-station observation   | Operational workflow                           | Observable workflow, transaction sequence, approximate duration, observed exceptions | Internal thoughts or motivations                          |
| Short survey               | Broader self-reported patterns                 | Frequencies and patterns within the collected sample                                 | Verified causes or detailed behavioral explanations       |
| Usability testing          | Future prototype evaluation                    | Task success, errors, comprehension, interaction problems                            | Real-world performance of the current national system     |

Usability testing belongs to a later phase after the proposed prototype has been developed.

# 8. Interview Plan

Interviews will use semi-structured, open-ended questions.

Questions should avoid embedding an assumed cause or negative experience.

For example:

**Avoid:**

> "Does poor internet make your QR code fail?"

**Use:**

> "Can you describe what happens when the QR code cannot be verified?"

---

## 8.1 Citizen / Vehicle User Interview Guide

### Context

1. What type of vehicle do you operate?
2. How often do you normally purchase fuel?
3. Can you describe what you usually do before visiting a fuel station?

### Registration

4. Can you describe how you first registered for the National Fuel Pass?
5. Which parts of registration, if any, required additional effort or assistance?
6. What information did you need to provide?

### Ownership / Recovery

7. Have you ever needed to update registration details because of a vehicle ownership or contact-number change?
8. If yes, what did you do first?
9. What happened during the process?
10. Where did you seek help, if anywhere?

### QR Usage

11. How do you normally access or present your QR code at a fuel station?
12. Can you describe what happens when the QR code is not immediately verified?

### Quota

13. How do you check your remaining fuel quota?
14. How do you understand when your quota becomes available again?

### Support

15. If something goes wrong with the Fuel Pass, what do you normally do?
16. What information would make it easier for you to resolve the problem?

---

# 9. Interview Guide — Ownership and Recovery

For participants who have experienced an ownership or contact-number issue:

1. What situation caused you to update or re-register your vehicle?
2. What did you try first?
3. What information or documentation were you asked to provide?
4. Was there anything that was unclear during the process?
5. Which support channels did you use?
6. What happened after you contacted support?
7. Was the issue resolved? If so, how?
8. If not, where did the process stop?

No participant should be asked to disclose real NIC numbers, OTPs, passwords, or other sensitive credentials.

---

# 10. Interview Guide — Digital Access and Accessibility

1. What device do you normally use for online services?
2. How comfortable are you completing online forms?
3. Do you normally complete these tasks yourself or with assistance?
4. If someone assists you, what part of the process do they help with?
5. Which language do you prefer for digital service information?
6. Have you encountered anything that made information difficult to read or understand?
7. What would make the service easier for you to use?

These questions investigate possible accessibility issues without assuming that a barrier exists.

---

# 11. Interview Guide — Fuel-Station Personnel

### Workflow

1. Can you walk me through the normal process when a vehicle arrives for fueling?
2. Where does QR verification occur within that process?
3. What equipment or interface do you use for verification?
4. What information does the system provide during a normal transaction?

### Exceptions

5. Can you describe what happens when a QR code cannot be verified?
6. What do you normally do next?
7. What happens when the system displays an error?
8. What happens when there is a connectivity or equipment problem?

### Queue / Volume

9. Does the workflow change when there is a larger queue?
10. If it changes, what specifically changes?

The researcher should record the participant's actual description rather than introduce assumptions about workload, fatigue, or responsibility.

---

# 12. Interview Guide — Station Managers / Support Personnel

### Operations

1. What types of NFP-related questions or problems are brought to you?
2. How are these problems normally handled?
3. Which issues can be resolved at the station?
4. Which issues require another support channel?

### Escalation

5. What information is normally required when escalating a problem?
6. What types of cases take longer to resolve?
7. What information would make these cases easier to handle?

For support personnel, questions should focus on their actual documented role and experience.

# 13. Contextual Inquiry Plan

Contextual inquiry will combine observation with short questions during appropriate pauses.

## 13.1 Citizen Context

Where ethically and practically possible, researchers may observe users completing relevant NFP tasks and ask short clarification questions such as:

* "What are you looking for here?"
* "What did you expect to happen?"
* "What did you do next?"
* "Can you explain why you chose that option?"

Researchers should not interrupt safety-critical activities.

---

## 13.2 Fuel-Station Context

Where permission is obtained, researchers may observe the service process around:

* vehicle arrival
* QR presentation
* verification
* fuel dispensing
* transaction completion
* exception handling
* queue movement

Observation should not interfere with station operations or participant safety.

# 16. Ethics and Privacy

All primary research will follow appropriate academic research and privacy practices.

## 16.1 Voluntary Participation

Participation must be voluntary.

Participants should be informed about:

* the purpose of the research
* what participation involves
* how information will be used
* their right to decline questions
* their right to stop participating

---

## 16.2 Sensitive Information

Researchers must not collect unnecessary sensitive information.

The following must not be recorded as research data:

* real NIC numbers
* passport numbers
* active OTPs
* passwords
* payment-card information
* authentication credentials
* unnecessary personal phone numbers
* home addresses

Vehicle or registration identifiers should also be avoided or anonymized where they are not necessary for the research.

---

## 16.3 Synthetic Identifiers

Research records should use synthetic identifiers such as:

* `CITIZEN-01`
* `CITIZEN-RECOVERY-02`
* `STATION-01`
* `SUPPORT-01`
* `OBS-URBAN-01`

Participant names should not be used in the analysis dataset.

---

## 16.4 Recording and Data Retention

If interviews are recorded, participants must provide appropriate consent.

Recordings and research notes should be stored securely with access restricted to the research team.

Where recordings are used, they should be deleted after transcription verification unless participants have explicitly consented to longer retention and such retention is appropriate for the academic project.

## 18.1 Qualitative Analysis

Interview and observation data will be coded according to:

* research domain
* task stage
* reported difficulty
* observed behavior
* workaround
* support interaction
* exception
* accessibility consideration
* contextual factor

Inductive themes may be added when supported by the collected data.

---

## 18.2 Quantitative Analysis

Survey responses may be summarized using:

* counts
* percentages
* distributions
* cross-tabulation where appropriate

Because the survey is expected to use an academic convenience/recruitment sample, results should not automatically be generalized to the entire Sri Lankan population.

---

## 18.3 Contradiction Tracking

Where different evidence sources appear to disagree, the discrepancy should be documented.

For example:

* interview: participant reports that QR verification is usually immediate
* observation: multiple attempts are observed during a particular transaction

The researcher should record both pieces of evidence and investigate the contextual difference rather than automatically declaring one source correct.

---

## 18.4 Triangulation

Important design-driving findings should, where feasible, be supported by more than one independent source.

Possible combinations include:

* interview + observation
* interview + survey
* observation + survey
* primary research + authoritative desk research

Triangulation does not require every finding to have multiple sources, but major design decisions should avoid relying on a single unverified assumption where additional validation is feasible.

# 19. Evidence Classification

All future findings will use the following classification:

| Tag         | Classification      | Definition                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **E**       | Evidence            | Directly documented, observed, or verified information |
| **I**       | Inference           | Logical interpretation derived from evidence           |
| **RH**      | Research Hypothesis | Unverified condition requiring investigation           |
| **DH**      | Design Hypothesis   | Proposed solution requiring validation                 |
| **TD**      | Technical Decision  | Engineering implementation decision                    |
| **UNKNOWN** | Unknown             | Information not established or verified                |

A statement should not be upgraded from **RH** to **E** merely because it appears repeatedly in interviews. The strength and context of the evidence must be considered.


# 22. Limitations

## 22.1 Academic Scope

This research supports an academic UX Engineering proposal and React MVP. It is not an officially commissioned redesign of the National Fuel Pass system.

## 22.2 Access Constraints

Access to institutional stakeholders, fuel stations, support personnel, and specific user groups may be limited.

Where direct access is unavailable, authoritative public documentation will be used and the limitation will be explicitly recorded.

## 22.3 Sampling Constraints

The proposed qualitative and survey targets are practical academic research targets and are not nationally representative samples.

## 22.4 Self-Report Bias

Interview and survey responses represent participant reports and may differ from observed behavior.

## 22.5 Observation Constraints

Station observations may capture only a limited number of locations, times, vehicle categories, and operational conditions.

## 22.6 Researcher Interpretation

Qualitative analysis involves researcher interpretation. Coding decisions and major inferences should therefore be documented and traceable to source evidence.

---

# 23. Remaining Unknowns

The following areas remain insufficiently established and should not be presented as facts:

* **UNKNOWN-01:** The detailed current technical architecture connecting public-facing NFP services, station systems, and relevant government systems.
* **UNKNOWN-02:** The precise error messages and technical states encountered on current station verification equipment.
* **UNKNOWN-03:** The frequency and distribution of different QR presentation methods among current users.
* **UNKNOWN-04:** The specific causes of QR verification failures in real-world station environments.
* **UNKNOWN-05:** The extent to which device, lighting, connectivity, or other environmental conditions affect verification outcomes.
* **UNKNOWN-06:** The actual distribution of smartphone, feature-phone, or other access methods among current users.
* **UNKNOWN-07:** The frequency of ownership/contact-number recovery problems within the broader current user population.
* **UNKNOWN-08:** Variation in station workflows across different locations and operators.
* **UNKNOWN-09:** The current responsibilities and technical roles of individual organizations where these are not established by authoritative current sources.

# 26. Conclusion

This document establishes the primary-research framework for the National Fuel Pass UX redesign.
